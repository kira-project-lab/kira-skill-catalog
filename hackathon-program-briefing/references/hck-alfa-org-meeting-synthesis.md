## Recommended 5 blocks for a participant org meeting

1. **Purpose and expected result**
   - This is a practical exam / ML competition block in the Alfa-Bank × HSE magistracy context.
   - Participants should produce a defensible technical result, not only a leaderboard score.

2. **Participant journey and calendar**
   - Kickoff/org meeting → registration/access check → case presentation → working period → Q&A/lectures → deadline → defense/results.
   - State Moscow time and hard deadlines.
   - For HCK-ALFA: verify the 12.07 vs 13.07 conflict before finalizing slides.

3. **Case, work format, and expected result**
   - Explain what will be given at case start, what teams/individuals do, and what a good solution should show.
   - If final case is missing, do not invent it; say it will be published at the scheduled start.

4. **Submission and platform**
   - Platform/access, registration, where materials live, submission deadline.
   - Working standard: keep code/repo/archive, README, dependency versions, data/source notes, model/AI notes if applicable, and a short presentation.

5. **Evaluation, checks, defense, and communication**
   - Metric/leaderboard if applicable, plus review/defense.
   - Reproducibility, no forbidden external data/network use, no copied solutions, no manual submission hacking.
   - Support channels, Q&A windows, where clarifications are published, and immediate next actions after the meeting.

## What to exclude from participant slides

- Commercial budget/smeta and contract terms.
- Internal statuses like `Not started` in Run of Show.
- Reviewer acronyms and internal anti-cheat KPI calculations.
- Full OrangeHack business model or agency pitch.
- Facts from Alpha Campus Start or Academy AI as if they are current Alfa Magistracy facts.

## Good answer shape

When Maxim asks for a compact meeting structure, answer directly with the block list and 1–3 bullets per block. Add a short timing recommendation only if helpful. Mention excluded topics separately at the end.
